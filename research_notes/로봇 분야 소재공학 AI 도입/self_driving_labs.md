## 1. Key systems worldwide and their results

### Takeaway
Since 2016 SDLs have gone from single-purpose closed loops (AFRL ARES, 2016) to mobile-robot chemists (Liverpool 2020/2024), solid-state inorganic synthesis labs (A-Lab 2023), and LLM-agent-driven labs (Coscientist/ChemCrow 2023–24, multi-agent systems 2025–26). In 2025–26 the field added national programs (Korea, Japan, Canada, US) and heavily funded startups (Lila, Periodic, Radical AI). The most famous "discovery" claim, A-Lab's 41 novel compounds, was publicly disputed in 2023–24 and formally corrected in Nature in January 2026.

### Cited Findings

**USA: Berkeley Lab / UC Berkeley A-Lab (inorganic powders)**
- [PR, Nature, 29 Nov 2023] The A-Lab claimed to synthesize **41 novel compounds out of 58 targets in 17 days** of continuous operation. Targets were air-stable inorganic powders identified via Materials Project and Google DeepMind computations. — [Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article); [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10700133); [OSTI](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2281696)
- [PR] Pipeline: recipes are generated by ML models trained on the literature (NLP-mined synthesis data), then refined through active learning (ARROWS3-style thermodynamic reasoning) when the first attempts fail. Phases are identified from XRD by ML models with automated refinement. — [OSTI/A-Lab paper](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2281696); [Ceder group page](https://ceder.berkeley.edu/research-areas/autonomous-experimentation-for-accelerated-materials-discovery/)
- [Press/critique, Dec 2023] Robert Palgrave (UCL) and Leslie Schoop (Princeton) argued that the automated XRD refinement was poor ("very bad, very beginner", per Palgrave) and that about two-thirds of the predicted compounds were ordered versions of already-known disordered compounds. Their conclusion was that no new materials had been discovered. — [Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article); [Nature News, "Robot chemist sparks row"](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-023-03956-w); [The Register, Jan 2024](https://www.theregister.com/2024/01/31/ai_chemistry_research_disputed/)
- [PR critique, PRX Energy, March 2024] Leeman, Schoop, Palgrave et al. formalized the critique. One summary says about two-thirds of the 43 compounds claimed as new were likely known. — [C&EN search summary / postcutoff](https://postcutoff.com/e/2023-11-29-a-lab-autonomous-synthesis-dispute/). Note: these secondary sources disagree on the count (41 vs. 43).
- [Author response, Dec 2023] Gerbrand Ceder (LinkedIn) added energy-dispersive spectroscopy data. He conceded that MnAgO2 and Mg3Ni3MnO8 were not new, and acknowledged that a human could do a better refinement, but argued the aim was to show what an autonomous lab can do. — [Ceder LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/regarding-our-recent-a-lab-article-gerbrand-ceder-0sz6c); [Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article)
- [PR correction, Nature, 19 Jan 2026] An author correction reframes "novel" to mean new **to the prediction platform**, not necessarily new to science. Search summaries say the platform reached the correct conclusion in **36 of 40** reported successes, with 4 inconclusive. One compound (Zn2Cr3FeO8) was removed because it had been in the training data. The paper was corrected, not retracted. Palgrave told C&EN that retraction may no longer be needed and that the paper is "now less interesting but not so flawed". Schoop was not fully satisfied. — [C&EN, Jan 2026](https://cen.acs.org/research-integrity/Nature-robot-chemist-paper-corrected/104/web/2026/01?sc=230901_cenrssfeed_eng_latestnewsrss_cen); [UMN experts listing of Author Correction](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/author-correction-an-autonomous-laboratory-for-the-accelerated-sy/); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4kb4s6pg)
  - **Conflict:** some secondary sources ([kingy.ai tracker](https://kingy.ai/mathematics-science-breakthrough-tracker/records/science-correction-2026-a-lab-autonomous-materials/), [postcutoff](https://postcutoff.com/e/2023-11-29-a-lab-autonomous-synthesis-dispute/)) describe the corrected result as "36 of 57 targets" and "36 inorganic (not novel) materials". - [Preprint, Apr 2026] **A-Lab GPSS**: a glovebox-integrated version for air-sensitive inorganic materials. Not yet peer-reviewed. — [alphaXiv 2604.11957](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.11957.md)
- [Press, Jan 2026] Ceder is now Chief Science Officer of the NYC startup **Radical AI**, which is building a self-driving materials lab. **UK: University of Liverpool (Andrew Cooper group), mobile robotic chemists**
- [PR, Nature 583:237–241, July 2020] Burger et al., "A mobile robotic chemist": a KUKA-type arm on a mobile base with a multipurpose gripper, navigating by laser scanning. It "automates the researcher, not the instruments". It ran **688 experiments in 8 days** in a 10-variable space using batched Bayesian optimization, and found photocatalyst mixtures for H2 evolution from water **6× more active** than the initial formulations. — [Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3095139/); [Chemistry World 2020](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/your-new-labmate-does-700-reactions-in-eight-days-and-its-a-robot/4012125.article); [Strathprints](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/74759/)
- [Press] Times Higher Education headlined the robot as "works 1,000 times faster" than human researchers. That is a press framing, not a figure from the paper. — [THE](https://www.stg.timeshighereducation.com/node/696379)
- [PR, Nature, Nov 2024; DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-08173-7] Dai, Cooper et al., "Autonomous mobile robots for exploratory synthetic chemistry". Two **1.75 m tall mobile robots** connect a Chemspeed automated synthesis platform, a UPLC-MS and a **benchtop NMR**, sharing ordinary lab equipment with humans. A heuristic decision-maker processes orthogonal (MS and NMR) data to decide which reactions to scale up or continue. It was applied to structural diversification chemistry, supramolecular host-guest chemistry and photochemical synthesis. The authors report decisions "comparable to a synthetic chemist" made in seconds rather than hours. Cooper noted the robots have less "contextual breadth" than a trained researcher. — [Liverpool news](https://news.liverpool.ac.uk/?p=123346); [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/11/241106132220.htm); [Chemspeed](https://www.chemspeed.com/news/autonomous-mobile-robots-for-exploratory-synthetic-chemistry/)

**USA: Argonne Polybot (electronic polymers)**
- [PR, Nature Communications, Feb 2025; DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-55655-3] Wang, Kim, Vriza et al., "Autonomous platform for solution processing of electronic polymers". Polybot sits at the Center for Nanoscale Materials. It used **importance-guided Bayesian optimization over a 7-dimensional processing space** (formulation, coating and post-processing) for PEDOT:PSS-type films. It reached transparent conductive films with **average conductivity > 4,500 S/cm**, comparable to the best achievable, plus low-defect scale-up recipes. The data were shared openly. — [ANL](https://www.anl.gov/article/selfdriving-lab-transforms-materials-discovery); [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/ai-driven-autonomous-lab-argonne-transforms-materials-discovery); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-024-55655-3.html)

**USA: Carnegie Mellon (Coscientist, Clio) and Emerald Cloud Lab**
- [PR, Nature 624:570–578, 20 Dec 2023; DOI 10.1038/s41586-023-06792-0] Boiko, MacKnight, Kline, Gomes, "Autonomous chemical research with large language models" (Coscientist). A GPT-4 "Planner" coordinates web search, documentation search, code execution and experiment automation. It found **6 fast-charging electrolytes in 2 work-days and 42 experiments**, a **6-fold time acceleration vs. random search** on the same robot. **Switzerland / IBM: ChemCrow (LLM agent with a cloud robot)**

### Inferences
- The strongest peer-reviewed quantified wins are in **optimization** within a defined space: Burger 6× activity, Clio 6× faster than random search, Polybot >4,500 S/cm, RoboChem better yields in ~80% of cases. Claims of **discovering new materials** (A-Lab) are where scrutiny has bitten hardest. The 2026 correction shows that validation (XRD refinement quality, novelty checks against ICSD) is the weak link of autonomy.
- The architecture has moved from fixed integrated workcells toward **mobile robots that share human equipment** (Liverpool, USTC) and **LLM orchestration layers** (Coscientist, ChemCrow, MARS, A-Lab GPSS, KRICT). Bayesian optimization remains the workhorse for the inner loop.
- Asian national programs (Korea, Japan) explicitly frame SDLs as a demographic and labour response as well as a scientific accelerator.

## 2. How the AI components are applied

### Takeaway
AI enters SDLs at five layers: (1) **target or hypothesis generation** (DFT/ML stability prediction, e.g., Materials Project and GNoME for A-Lab); (2) **recipe and experiment planning** (literature-trained NLP or LLM recipe models, Bayesian optimization or active learning); (3) **robotic execution and orchestration** (fixed workcells, mobile manipulators, cloud labs, LLM tool-use); (4) **automated characterization analysis** (ML XRD phase ID, NMR/MS interpretation, physics-informed microscopy); (5) **reasoning, agents and knowledge capture** (LLM multi-agent systems, natural-language priors).

### Cited Findings
- **Target selection and recipe generation:** A-Lab uses computed stable targets, literature-trained ML recipe proposals, and active learning to refine failed syntheses. — [OSTI A-Lab paper](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/2281696); A-Lab also synthesized materials predicted by Google DeepMind's models — [R&D World](https://www.rdworldonline.com/how-radical-ai-is-building-a-self-driving-materials-lab/)
- **Bayesian optimization / closed-loop optimization:** batched BO in Burger 2020 ([Liverpool](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3095139/)); Dragonfly BO in Clio ([Nature Comms](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-32938-1.pdf)); importance-guided BO over 7-D in Polybot ([ANL](https://www.anl.gov/article/selfdriving-lab-transforms-materials-discovery)); BO over >3M compositions in the USTC AI chemist ([CAS](https://english.cas.cn/newsroom/cas_media/202311/t20231115_643207.shtml)); multi-objective Pareto-front mapping in Ada ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08899)).
- **Active learning** is described as a crucial strategy to cut the number of required experiments. — [ResearchGate review 2026](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/413789542_AI-Guided_Self-Driving_Laboratories_for_Advanced_Materials_Discovery)
- **Automated data analysis:** ML-based XRD phase identification and refinement in A-Lab, whose quality was the main point of criticism ([Chemistry World](https://chemistryworld.com/news/new-analysis-raises-doubts-over-autonomous-labs-materials-discoveries/4018791.article)); orthogonal UPLC-MS and benchtop-NMR interpretation by a heuristic decision-maker in Liverpool 2024 ([Liverpool](https://news.liverpool.ac.uk/?p=123346)); in situ Raman as the objective in ARES ([Cambridge](https://core-prod.cambridgecore.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/73ED194B0E54A40536E218080D90D633/S1431927617001593a.pdf/autonomous_experimentation_applied_to_carbon_nanotube_synthesis.pdf)); physics-informed ML in autonomous SPM ([ORNL](https://www.ornl.gov/news/self-driving-microscopes-discover-shortcuts-new-materials)).
- **LLM agents:** Coscientist's GPT-4 Planner wrote code for the OT-2 and ECL ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733136)); ChemCrow used 18 tools on RoboRXN ([Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00832-8.pdf)); MARS used 19 agents and 16 tools ([Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-01-multi-agent-ai-robots-automate.html)); AutoLabs (Scientific Reports 2026) is a multi-agent system that plans steps, does stoichiometry via tool calls, self-validates, and emits the XML hardware file for a liquid handler ([Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-45593-z)); SynAgent (arXiv Sep 2026) ran 18 autonomous experiments on **LiCoO2 (001) thin-film deposition** and found a narrow optimal substrate-temperature window, presented as human-readable understanding ([arXiv 2609.18598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18598)); A-Lab GPSS uses agentic abductive and inductive reasoning ([alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.11957.md)).
- **Assessment of LLM agents** (Sept 2026 review): LLM agents are most useful for planning and tool coordination. Optimization, physical validity and safety still need specialist methods and explicit constraints. Reliable autonomy depends on data standards, interoperable tools, preserved experimental context and safeguards. — [Nanowerk spotlight (search summary)](https://www.nanowerk.com/spotlight/spotid=70238.php)
- **Orchestration software:** NIMS-OS/NIMO is open-source middleware that connects AI modules to robots, including legacy liquid handlers. — [NIMS MDR](https://mdr.nims.go.jp/datasets/05a74658-08b8-433a-8106-5c98dfb3a0bd?locale=en)
- **Robot hardware types:** mobile manipulators with laser navigation (Burger 2020; [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/your-new-labmate-does-700-reactions-in-eight-days-and-its-a-robot/4012125.article)); 1.75 m mobile robots sharing equipment (Dai 2024; [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/11/241106132220.htm)); glovebox-integrated solid-state lines (A-Lab GPSS); flow reactors (RoboChem; [UvA](https://hims.uva.nl/content/news/2024/01/autonomous-synthesis-robot-uses-ai-to-speed-up-chemical-discovery.html)); cloud labs (ECL, IBM RoboRXN); robotic arms with arc melter and furnace (KIMS); dual-arm LabDroid (RIKEN Maholo).
- **Human knowledge capture:** KRICT's conversational AI turns natural-language researcher expertise into planning priors. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1148248)

### Inferences
- In practice the "AI" in most peer-reviewed SDL successes is still sample-efficient black-box optimization (GP-based BO). LLMs mainly add flexible planning, code generation and tool glue, which matters most for generality, while reliability problems persist (ChemCrow's non-executable procedures).
- Automated characterization analysis, especially XRD phase ID for new solids, is the bottleneck that determines whether autonomous discovery claims stand up (the A-Lab lesson).

### Gaps
- No source retrieved on **robot motion or manipulation learning** (e.g., imitation learning for powder handling or vial manipulation) or on **digital twins** of SDLs with concrete metrics. Only the Osaka powder-grinding robot and RL-in-AM reviews (section 4) touch these.

### Inferences
- The defensible headline numbers are **order-of-magnitude reductions in experiments to reach an optimum** (e.g., 24 vs. ~10,000 at KIST, a press claim; 42 experiments at Clio) and **continuous multi-day operation**. Time-to-discovery claims such as "2,000 years → 6 weeks" are extrapolations and should not be cited as measured speedups.
- Reproducibility improvements are frequently asserted (RoboChem logs negative data; perovskite SDLs aim at reproducible manufacturing), but I found no head-to-head reproducibility statistic.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved with measured **cost reductions** (US$ per discovery) for any SDL.

- "Degree of autonomy" level schemes (L0–L5 analogies to autonomous driving) were not retrieved with a citation.

## 4. AI in robotic manufacturing and processing of materials (additive manufacturing, welding)

### Takeaway
ML-based closed-loop control in metal additive manufacturing (L-PBF, DED, WAAM) is active research, mostly at the level of **in situ monitoring, defect detection and parameter tuning**. Fully autonomous closed-loop robotic metal printing remains early-stage, and reinforcement learning work is still mostly in simulation.

### Cited Findings
- A Feb 2025 review of image-based monitoring in metal AM describes ML as the route to closed-loop, adaptive autonomous manufacturing. A Sept 2025 Advanced Powder Materials review says multi-physics effects and closed-loop control "have not yet been thoroughly investigated". — [search summary, MDPI Metals 15(9):966](https://mdpi.com/2075-4701/15/9/966); [Whiterose 2026 review PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/240849/1/1-s2.0-S1526612526004342-main.pdf)
- A 2026 Journal of Manufacturing Processes review finds that most RL work in **robotic AM** is confined to simulation, with only a small but growing subset doing closed-loop learning with digital twins or reduced-order models. — [Whiterose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/240849/1/1-s2.0-S1526612526004342-main.pdf)
- A 2025 Metals review of ML in DED/WAAM names limited closed-loop control and poor generalization across systems as key challenges. — [MDPI Metals](https://mdpi.com/2075-4701/15/9/966)
- [Preprint] A robotic wire-arc AM learning framework with line-scanner feedback improved bead height and width consistency vs. baselines. — [arXiv 2605.29144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29144)
- [Thesis, 2025] TU Delft applied ML closed-loop control to robotic clay 3D printing (UR5 arm with camera feedback). — [TU Delft repository](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:e4512920-3a13-4449-8b44-6243717a3ee4)
- "Smart additive manufacturing empowered by a closed-loop ML algorithm" is an earlier peer-reviewed example. — [MTMT record](https://m2.mtmt.hu/api/publication/30914466)
- I found no source linking AFRL ARES to additive manufacturing. ARES sits within the AFRL Materials and Manufacturing Directorate. — [AF.mil](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/982368/afrl-system-revolutionizes-research-process/)

### Inferences
- Manufacturing-side "autonomy" lags lab-scale SDLs. The bottleneck is generalizing learned controllers across machines and materials, and sim-to-real transfer.

### Gaps
- No quantified industrial deployments (scrap-rate reduction, yield) of ML-controlled robotic welding or AM were retrieved. AFRL's later ARES variants for AM (if any) are unverified.
